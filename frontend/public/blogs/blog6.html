Most homeowners in Visakhapatnam understand solar panels generate electricity — but very few understand how that electricity actually turns into a lower bill. The answer is a billing mechanism called net metering, and it is quietly the most misunderstood part of going solar. Get it, and you'll read your bill with total confidence. ✅ Quick Answer

Net metering means you are billed only for the net difference between the electricity you draw from the grid and the electricity your solar system sends back to it. A single bi-directional meter tracks both directions, any extra units you export carry forward as a credit to your next bill, and the meter itself is installed by APEPDCL at no separate cost under the PM Surya Ghar process.

1. What Is Net Metering? (Simple Explanation)

Think of net metering as a running tally between you and the electricity board. During the day, your solar panels generate power. Your home uses some of it directly — running fans, fridges, and lights off sunlight in real time. Whatever your panels generate but your home doesn't use at that moment is automatically exported to the grid.

  • Import — electricity you draw from the grid (at night, on cloudy days, or when your usage exceeds generation)
  • Export — surplus solar electricity sent back to the grid when generation exceeds your usage
  • Net units — import minus export, over the billing cycle, is what APEPDCL actually charges you for

You are never disconnected from the grid — net metering simply means the grid acts like a giant "battery" for your excess solar units, crediting them back to you instead of paying you in cash for every unit in real time.

2. How the Bi-Directional Meter Works

A regular electricity meter only counts in one direction — how much you consume. A bi-directional (net) meter has two separate registers: one that counts units flowing from the grid into your home, and one that counts units flowing from your solar system back into the grid.

☀️
Daytime Generation
Export Register

Your panels generate more than your home consumes. The surplus flows backward through the meter, and the export register counts these units.

🌙
Night & Low Sun
Import Register

With no solar generation, your home draws entirely from the grid as usual. These units are tracked on the import register, same as before solar.

⚖️
Billing Cycle End
Net Settlement

APEPDCL subtracts total exported units from total imported units for the cycle. You're billed only on the positive difference, if any.

🔌 Note: The meter itself does not "decide" anything — it only measures. The net settlement calculation happens on APEPDCL's billing system using the two register readings, exactly like how your regular consumption bill is calculated today.

3. Billing Cycle & Credit Carry-Forward Explained

APEPDCL bills residential net-metering customers on a cycle similar to a standard electricity bill — typically every 30 to 45 days depending on your division. What makes solar billing different is what happens to unused exported units.

Scenario What Happens
You export more units than you import this cycleSurplus units carry forward as a credit to your next bill
You import more units than you export this cycleYou're billed for the net shortfall, minus any carried-forward credit
Credit remains unused at financial year-endSettled per the applicable annual true-up policy (payout or lapse, per current AP regulation)
Your system is sized close to your average consumptionBills typically settle near the fixed/service-charge minimum most months

This is why correctly sizing your solar system to your household's actual consumption pattern matters — an undersized system leaves you paying more than necessary, while an oversized system accumulates credits that may not be paid out at full retail value at year-end.

6. Net Metering vs Gross Metering

Andhra Pradesh residential rooftop solar overwhelmingly uses net metering, but it's worth understanding the alternative to know why:

✅ Net Metering
  • You use your own solar power first, at its full retail value
  • Only the surplus is exported and credited
  • Simpler single bill, single meter, single settlement
  • Best fit for most Vizag homes under PM Surya Ghar
⚠️ Gross Metering
  • 100% of solar generation is sold to the DISCOM at a fixed feed-in tariff
  • You separately buy 100% of your consumption at the retail rate
  • Feed-in tariff is usually lower than the retail rate you pay
  • Mainly relevant for large generators, not typical residential rooftops

7. If Your Bill Looks Wrong

A few checks before assuming there's an error:

  • Confirm both readings appear — your bill should show separate import and export unit figures, not just one combined number
  • Check for a recent weather run — a string of overcast or monsoon days genuinely lowers export units for that cycle
  • Verify carried-forward credit is reflected — if last cycle had a surplus, this cycle's bill should show that credit applied
  • Rule out a generation drop first — a sudden fall in export units can simply mean panels need cleaning, not a billing error
  • Contact your installer before the DISCOM if the numbers still don't add up — most metering queries are resolved faster through your installer's liaison with APEPDCL

8. Frequently Asked Questions

What is net metering in simple terms?
Net metering is a billing system where the electricity your solar panels generate but don't use is sent back to the grid, and you get credited for those units. Your bill is based on the "net" of what you imported from the grid minus what you exported to it.
Do unused solar units carry forward every month in Visakhapatnam?
Yes. Under APEPDCL's net metering policy, excess exported units carry forward as a credit to your next billing cycle. Any credit still unused at the end of the annual settlement period is typically paid out or lapses at a nominal rate, depending on the applicable state regulation at the time.
Do I have to pay for the bi-directional meter?
Under the PM Surya Ghar scheme, the bi-directional (net) meter is installed by APEPDCL as part of the subsidy-linked rooftop solar process, so residential applicants typically do not pay separately for it. Installation timelines can vary by division.
What is the difference between net metering and gross metering?
In net metering, you are billed only for the difference between what you import and export — you effectively use your own solar power first. In gross metering, all solar generation is sold to the DISCOM at a fixed feed-in tariff, and you separately buy all your consumption from the grid at the retail rate. Most residential Vizag homeowners benefit more from net metering.
Why does my bill still show some amount after installing solar?
You'll still be billed for fixed/service charges, any consumption beyond what your system generated, and units drawn at night or during low-sun periods. Net metering reduces your bill significantly but rarely brings it to exactly zero unless your system is oversized relative to consumption.
